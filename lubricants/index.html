What are automotive and industrial lubricants?

A lubricant is a material applied to surfaces to reduce friction or wear when those surfaces move relative to one another. Lubricating oil is a liquid lubricant; a lubricating grease contains a lubricating fluid held in a thickener structure. In vehicles and industrial equipment, the application determines the required product. An engine, a transmission and a compressor can need different lubricants even when a description or grade number looks similar.

References: NLGI · American Petroleum Institute · ExxonMobil / Mobil

Which lubricant category fits the application?

For engine lubrication, distinguish passenger car engine oils from heavy-duty diesel engine oils for commercial vehicles and off-highway equipment. Their performance requirements should be checked against the engine documentation.

For transmissions and automotive gears, identify the transmission or axle and its required fluid. ATF, CVT and DCT descriptions refer to different application requirements; a multi-vehicle label does not establish universal suitability.

For industrial equipment, identify hydraulic, industrial gear or compressor service. Bearing and multipurpose greases form another lubricant category. Use the relevant category page to narrow the application before asking about an individual reference.

References: American Petroleum Institute · ACEA · ACEA · ExxonMobil / Mobil · ExxonMobil / Mobil · ExxonMobil / Mobil · ExxonMobil / Mobil · NLGI

Read the grade in its application context

SAE engine-oil viscosity grades, automotive gear-lubricant grades, industrial ISO VG grades and NLGI grease consistency describe different classifications. None is a complete product specification. Start with the equipment requirement, then check the exact product's current technical information. The selection guide explains these grade systems and the distinction between a performance statement and a formal manufacturer approval.

References: SAE International · SAE International · NLGI · ExxonMobil / Mobil

Identify the brand and product reference

If you have a preferred product, include its full name and reference rather than a brand alone. SINOPEC and Great Wall Lubricants can be identified in an enquiry. Brand names identify requested products, not authorised distribution. For technical review, request the relevant TDS/PDS; for handling and safety information, request the SDS. Keep any manufacturer approval wording specific to the documented product and grade.

References: ExxonMobil / Mobil · ExxonMobil / Mobil

Common questions

Which lubricants can I enquire about at DUNE ENERGY GLOBAL?

You can enquire about passenger car engine oils, heavy-duty diesel engine oils, hydraulic, industrial gear and compressor oils, transmission and automotive gear oils, and lubricating greases. Availability and specifications are confirmed by quotation.

Is lubricating oil the same as lubricating grease?

No. Lubricating oil is liquid; grease contains lubricating fluid within a thickener structure. The equipment's lubrication requirement determines which product type is appropriate.

Can I choose a lubricant by viscosity grade alone?

No. Check the application, performance specification and any required manufacturer approval as well as the grade. A shared grade does not establish interchangeability.

How do I request a quotation for a US or Canadian destination?

Send the product or requirement, quantity, packaging and delivery destination to info@energydune.com. Include the requested date. Supply and delivery terms require confirmation in the quotation.

Do the brand names indicate authorised distribution?

No. SINOPEC and Great Wall Lubricants identify products customers may request. Their names do not imply authorised distribution, brand affiliation or confirmed availability of a particular product.

Related information

Passenger car engine oils

DUNE ENERGY GLOBAL handles enquiries for passenger car engine oils by product reference and vehicle requirement. A useful request identifies the required viscosity and performance specification together; a familiar brand or viscosity grade alone is not enough to establish suitability.

Heavy-duty diesel engine oils

Heavy-duty diesel engine-oil enquiries need more than a viscosity grade. DUNE ENERGY GLOBAL accepts requests for commercial vehicles and off-highway equipment using the engine requirement, preferred product and purchasing details. Identify the engine and any emissions-system requirements so the enquiry describes the intended application clearly.

Industrial lubricants

DUNE ENERGY GLOBAL accepts enquiries for hydraulic oils, industrial gear oils and compressor oils. Start with the equipment and its lubrication requirement, then identify the requested brand or reference. An industrial viscosity grade helps describe the request, but it does not make different application types interchangeable.

Transmission and gear oils

Transmission-oil enquiries should identify the unit being lubricated, not only the vehicle. DUNE ENERGY GLOBAL accepts requests for ATF, CVT, DCT and automotive gear oils by product reference and manufacturer requirement. The transmission or axle specification is the starting point for describing the correct product.

Lubricating greases

DUNE ENERGY GLOBAL accepts enquiries for bearing and multipurpose greases in industrial and commercial applications. A useful grease request combines consistency, equipment and operating conditions. If you are replacing a product, identify the existing grease as well as the proposed reference so compatibility can be considered.

Selection guide

A clear lubricant enquiry describes the equipment requirement and the requested product separately. Start with the machine or vehicle documentation, identify the relevant viscosity or consistency system, then record the performance specification. Brand, quantity, packaging and destination complete the commercial request; they do not establish technical suitability.
