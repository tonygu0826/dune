NLGI describes consistency

The NLGI grade classifies grease consistency using worked penetration. It is not the viscosity grade of the base oil, nor a complete measure of performance. Two NLGI 2 greases can differ in base-oil viscosity, thickener, water resistance and intended service. Choose the required grade from the equipment or lubrication-system specification rather than assuming one consistency suits every application.

References: NLGI

Consider the existing grease

NLGI defines incompatibility by adverse physical or performance properties in a grease mixture. A common consistency number does not establish compatibility. Before a changeover, record the exact existing and proposed products and follow equipment and lubricant-manufacturer guidance. Include relevant speed, load, temperature and water exposure when these affect the application.

References: NLGI · ExxonMobil / Mobil

What to include in your enquiry

  • Bearing, equipment or lubrication-system application.
  • Required NLGI consistency grade and manufacturer specification.
  • Preferred brand and exact grease reference.
  • Existing grease, including thickener and base-oil details if known.
  • Relevant load, speed, temperature and water exposure.
  • Quantity, packaging, destination and TDS/PDS or SDS request.

Common questions

Does NLGI 2 mean a grease has the right oil viscosity?

No. NLGI 2 is a consistency grade. Check the grease's base-oil viscosity and application requirements separately in the product's technical information.

Can two greases with the same NLGI grade be mixed?

Do not assume compatibility from the NLGI grade. Identify both products and check the relevant manufacturer guidance or compatibility assessment before mixing or changing over.
