How to specify a lubricant for an enquiry

A clear lubricant enquiry describes the equipment requirement and the requested product separately. Start with the machine or vehicle documentation, identify the relevant viscosity or consistency system, then record the performance specification. Brand, quantity, packaging and destination complete the commercial request; they do not establish technical suitability.

Updated 2 November 2026

Applications

  • Automotive lubricant purchasing
  • Commercial vehicle maintenance
  • Industrial lubrication enquiries

1. Identify what needs lubrication

State whether the request concerns a passenger car engine, heavy-duty diesel engine, transmission, industrial system or grease application. Include the equipment make and model or the exact requirement copied from its manual. When replacing an existing product, provide its full reference; a partial name or colour is not a reliable specification.

References: American Petroleum Institute · ExxonMobil / Mobil · NLGI

2. Use the correct grade system

SAE J300 classifies engine-oil viscosity; SAE J306 covers automotive gear-lubricant viscosity. These are different scales. ISO VG describes industrial lubricant viscosity at 40 °C. NLGI describes grease consistency, not the viscosity of its base oil. A grade number helps identify one property, but does not by itself establish performance or interchangeability.

References: ExxonMobil / Mobil · ExxonMobil / Mobil · NLGI · SAE International · SAE International

3. Keep performance and approval wording exact

For engine oils, record the required API category or ACEA designation and any manufacturer requirement. Do not assume that a category with a later-looking designation is universally interchangeable: API's FA-4 guidance is one clear exception. For any application, distinguish a formal manufacturer approval from statements such as recommended for or meets requirements. Check the exact product and current documentation rather than transferring a claim from another grade or product family.

References: American Petroleum Institute · ACEA · ACEA · ExxonMobil / Mobil

4. Request the appropriate documents

A technical or product data sheet, TDS/PDS, describes the product, typical properties and stated applications or specifications. A safety data sheet, SDS, addresses hazards, handling and safety information. They serve different purposes. Ask for the current documents for the requested reference; an SDS does not establish a vehicle approval, and typical properties are not a substitute for the equipment requirement.

References: ExxonMobil / Mobil · ExxonMobil / Mobil · ExxonMobil / Mobil

5. Complete the purchasing details

Add the preferred brand, quantity with units, pack size, destination and requested date. Indicate whether an exact product is required or an alternative may be reviewed. An enquiry is a request for consideration; availability, specifications and commercial terms need confirmation in the quotation.

Viscosity, consistency and performance are different

DesignationWhat it describesWhat it does not confirm

What to include in your enquiry

  • Application and equipment requirement.
  • Exact product or preferred brand, if known.
  • Viscosity or consistency grade plus performance specification.
  • Required approval and supporting documentation.
  • Quantity, packaging, destination and requested date.

Common questions

What should I send if I do not know the product reference?

Provide the equipment make and model, the lubricant requirement from its manual and details of the existing product if available. Avoid guessing equivalence from a viscosity grade alone.

Does this guide confirm a product is approved or in stock?

No. It explains how to describe a request. Individual product suitability, approval status and supply must be checked for the specific enquiry and confirmed in the relevant documentation and quotation.

Related information

Passenger car engine oils

DUNE ENERGY GLOBAL handles enquiries for passenger car engine oils by product reference and vehicle requirement. A useful request identifies the required viscosity and performance specification together; a familiar brand or viscosity grade alone is not enough to establish suitability.

Heavy-duty diesel engine oils

Heavy-duty diesel engine-oil enquiries need more than a viscosity grade. DUNE ENERGY GLOBAL accepts requests for commercial vehicles and off-highway equipment using the engine requirement, preferred product and purchasing details. Identify the engine and any emissions-system requirements so the enquiry describes the intended application clearly.

Industrial lubricants

DUNE ENERGY GLOBAL accepts enquiries for hydraulic oils, industrial gear oils and compressor oils. Start with the equipment and its lubrication requirement, then identify the requested brand or reference. An industrial viscosity grade helps describe the request, but it does not make different application types interchangeable.

Transmission and gear oils

Transmission-oil enquiries should identify the unit being lubricated, not only the vehicle. DUNE ENERGY GLOBAL accepts requests for ATF, CVT, DCT and automotive gear oils by product reference and manufacturer requirement. The transmission or axle specification is the starting point for describing the correct product.

Lubricating greases

DUNE ENERGY GLOBAL accepts enquiries for bearing and multipurpose greases in industrial and commercial applications. A useful grease request combines consistency, equipment and operating conditions. If you are replacing a product, identify the existing grease as well as the proposed reference so compatibility can be considered.
